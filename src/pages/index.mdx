<h2 style="text-align: left"> Abstract </h2>

Robotic systems have adopted throwing skills to enable fast and far-reaching object transportation beyond traditional pick-and-place operations. However, prehensile (grasp-based) throwing limits the range of manipulable objects to those that are feasible to grasp. In contrast, achieving non-prehensile (grasp-free) throwing requires precise control of inertial and frictional forces. In this work, we propose learning a non-prehensile throwing policy via reinforcement learning. Our Markov Decision Process (MDP) formulation enables offline planning of throwing trajectories for random objects—including heavy (790 g) and large (20x20x28 cm) ones—to random target positions as far as 3.5 m and as high as 1.8 m, while operating at the robot’s physical limits (5 m/s). Our simulation-trained policy is transferred to the real world in a zero-shot manner, achieving an average success rate of 85% across different objects and targets. Furthermore, our results demonstrate the policy’s robustness to modeling uncertainties and its generalization to unseen objects, including cylindrical cans and partially-filled bottles.

<h2 style="text-align: left">   Method   </h2>

We formulate the problem as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and deploy the policy as an offline planner ensuring sim-to-real transferability.

**(1) Markov Decision Process:**
<section class>
  <div class="box" style="background-color: #f5f5f5; border-left: 4px solid #df3f3cff; padding: 10px; font-size: 0.5rem; margin-left: 0;">
      <p>**Observation Space:** For offline planning, we do not use explicit object state. Instead, we assume that the robot state (joint positions and velocities) and object model are sufficient for planning open-loop throwing trajectories. An asymmetric actor-critic architecture is employed, where the critic receives privileged information during training.</p>
  </div>
</section>
<section class>
  <div class="box" style="background-color: #f5f5f5; border-left: 4px solid #df3f3cff; padding: 10px; font-size: 0.5rem; margin-left: 0;">
      <p>**Action Space:** An acceleration-based action is well-suited for smooth position/velocity trajectories and control frequency upsampling. The 20 Hz acceleration trajectory is upsampled to 120/480 simulated/real velocity commanded trajectories.</p>
  </div>
</section>
      <LaTeX formula="q_t = q_{t-1} + \dot{q}_{t-1}dt + \frac{1}{2} \ddot{q}_t dt^2 \;\; , \;\; \dot{q}_t = \dot{q}_{t-1} + \ddot{q}_{t}dt" />

<section class>
  <div class="box" style="background-color: #f5f5f5; border-left: 4px solid #df3f3cff; padding: 10px; font-size: 0.5rem; margin-left: 0;">
      <p>**Reward Formulation:** We design a weighted reward function combining live, dense, sparse, and regularization terms to promote accurate throws while discouraging undesired behaviors such as double throws, excessive projectile height, and high joint jerk.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class>
  <div class="box" style="background-color: #f5f5f5; border-left: 4px solid #df3f3cff; padding: 10px; font-size: 0.5rem; margin-left: 0;">
      <p>**Initial State Distribution:** We randomize the object model (cuboids) to cover a wide range of sizes, masses, and friction coefficients, including both small/large and light/heavy objects. Target positions are sampled to span long distances (up to 350 cm) and high elevations (up to 180 cm).</p>
  </div>
</section>

**(2) Deployment & Planning:**

<section class>
  <div class="box" style="background-color: #f5f5f5; border-left: 4px solid #df3f3cff; padding: 10px; font-size: 0.5rem; margin-left: 0;">
      <p>**Offline Trajectoroy Genenration:** Given object model, target position, and initial joint state, the acceleration trajectory is generated recursively by updating the joint acceleration, velocity, and position. Such workflow does not have access to groundtruth simuated/real states mitigating sensory noise and control latenncies.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class>
  <div class="box" style="background-color: #f5f5f5; border-left: 4px solid #df3f3cff; padding: 10px; font-size: 0.5rem; margin-left: 0;">
      <p>**Post-Throw Velocity Damping:** After the throw is detected or predicted—using a regressor trained on object model, target position, and initial joint state—a PD controller is applied to smoothly dampen joint velocities, ensuring stable robot behavior post-release.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class>
  <div class="box" style="background-color: #f5f5f5; border-left: 4px solid #df3f3cff; padding: 10px; font-size: 0.5rem; margin-left: 0;">
      <p>**Sim-to-Real Transfer:** We employ population-based system identification to tune simulated actuator gains, ensuring that the position and velocity profiles of predefined trajectories closely match those observed on the real robot.</p>
  </div>
</section>
